## Introduction

Walk into any electronics shop in Nairobi and you will see two types of laptops side by side: brand new machines in sealed boxes, and ex-UK laptops that look almost new but cost significantly less. The price difference can be Ksh 20,000 or more for a similar spec.

So what is the real story? Are ex-UK laptops worth buying, or is brand new always the safer choice? This guide gives you an honest answer.

---

## What Is an Ex-UK Laptop?

An ex-UK laptop is a used laptop that was previously owned in the United Kingdom — usually by a business, school, government department or large organisation. When those organisations upgrade their equipment (which happens on a regular cycle), the old machines are collected, tested, refurbished if needed, wiped clean and resold.

The result is a laptop that:
- Was built to business-grade standards (not budget consumer quality)
- Has been professionally used, not abused
- Has had its data wiped and software reinstalled
- Is sold at a significantly lower price than a new equivalent

Ex-UK stock is not the same as "secondhand" in the local marketplace sense. The machines go through a process before they reach Kenya, and reputable dealers like MobDeals vet what they stock.

## When to Buy Ex-UK

An ex-UK laptop makes sense if you:

**Want maximum performance for your money.** A Ksh 27,000 ex-UK ThinkPad T470s with an i5, 8GB RAM and a 256GB SSD is a more capable machine than a brand new laptop at the same price. Business laptops are built to a different standard than consumer machines — better keyboards, sturdier hinges, better screens and longer lifespan.

**Are buying for school or work.** For Microsoft Office, Google Docs, Zoom, emails and research, a 7th-generation Core i5 with 8GB RAM is completely sufficient. You do not need the latest chip for these tasks.

**Are buying on a budget.** If Ksh 20,000–30,000 is your range, you will get far better hardware from ex-UK stock than from brand new machines at the same price.

**Are comfortable buying from a reputable dealer.** This is the key condition. Ex-UK laptops are only worth buying when the dealer vets their stock, is transparent about the condition and offers a warranty. At MobDeals we check each unit and provide warranty information before purchase.

## When to Buy Brand New

A brand new laptop is the better choice if you:

**Need the latest software compatibility.** Some software — particularly in design, engineering or data science — benefits from newer processors and GPU support.

**Want a full manufacturer warranty.** Brand new machines come with a one-year warranty from HP, Lenovo, Dell or Apple. Ex-UK dealer warranties are shorter.

**Are buying for gaming or video editing.** These tasks demand more from the hardware, and current-generation processors and dedicated GPUs make a real difference.

**Value a pristine cosmetic condition.** Brand new means exactly that — no previous owner, no marks, no history.

**Are buying a MacBook.** Apple MacBooks hold their value differently and the secondhand MacBook market requires more careful evaluation. Brand new is simpler.

## Questions to Ask Before Buying an Ex-UK Laptop

1. What is the battery health? (Ask for a percentage or a cycle count)
2. Has the screen been tested? Any dead pixels or brightness issues?
3. What warranty does the dealer provide?
4. Has the hard drive been wiped and the OS reinstalled?
5. Are all ports working — USB, HDMI, headphone jack?
